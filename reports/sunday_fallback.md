# Why feature preferences, activation locations and class attribution differ

This is the revised fallback topic, not an approved or published post.

An optimized channel stimulus answers: what bounded synthetic input excites this
channel under a particular objective and regularization? A real-image activation map
answers: where does that channel respond on this image? A peak-channel input gradient
shows local infinitesimal sensitivity, not the training event that created the feature.

True-species Grad-CAM instead weights spatial activations using derivatives of one
class logit. Equal-area occlusion measures changes in the true-species-minus-other
logit margin under an artificial masking intervention. Their targets are related but
different, so agreement can be informative and disagreement is worth reporting.

A theoretical receptive-field box is possible architectural support, not proof that
all enclosed pixels contributed equally. ImageNet features may already be present
before fine-tuning. Synthetic patterns are not reconstructed memories, attractive
heatmaps do not certify named detectors, and none of these views proves robustness.

Use fixed images, recorded channel-selection rules, raw response values, randomized
controls and counterexamples. Keep photo-containing figures local until the separate
license/attribution and public-sharing gates are satisfied.
